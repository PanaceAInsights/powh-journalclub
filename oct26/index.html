CME, Reviews & Guidelines for the October 2026 meeting

Scope. Every article indexed in PubMed/MEDLINE for JAAD 93(4) & 93(5), Australas J Dermatol 66(7) & 66(8), and Br J Dermatol 193(4) & 193(5) was retrieved (312 records), then filtered to continuing-medical-education articles, narrative and clinical reviews, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, scoping reviews, guidelines and consensus statements. Section headings (Continuing Medical Education, Clinical Reviews, Review Article, Clinical Guidelines, Systematic Review…) were read from each publisher's own table of contents, since PubMed does not carry them. Original research, case reports, correspondence and editorials fall outside this month's scope.

Worth flagging. The Australasian Journal of Dermatology has no October issue. Volume 66 runs Feb, Mar, May, Jun, Aug, Sep, Nov and Dec, so 66(7) is November and, as when July was missing, the next number (66/8, December) fills the gap. Expect those eight December papers to recur in the next edition. JAAD's November issue has no Clinical Reviews section, which is why it contributes only three papers. Headline items this round: two Australian alopecia areata statements (the updated expert consensus on systemic treatment, and eDelphi criteria for subsidised JAK inhibitors), the Australasian College of Dermatologists' 2025 teledermatology guidelines, proposed national minimum standards for phototherapy and PUVA services, part two (treatment) of the ISCL/EORTC/USCLC paediatric mycosis fungoides consensus, and the two-paper BJD reclassification of epidermal differentiation disorders that follows September's palmoplantar instalment.

Papers without an abstract. Seven brief reports have no journal abstract. Two are open access, so each carries a synopsis compiled from the full text. Four are paywalled, so each carries only a scope note drawn from the publisher's free preview, and their results are left for the full text. One AJD research letter has neither.

Left out on purpose. Hybrids and viewpoints that sit outside the scope: AJD's Mohs surgery audit from Royal North Shore Hospital (original research with a review of the Australian literature), the yellow nested melanoma case with literature review, JAAD's viewpoint on interferon-gamma release assay retesting during biologic therapy, its two Dermatology Images atlases (SJS/TEN, and dominant dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa across skin types), and BJD's perspective on the EMA evaluation of nemolizumab.
